# Historical Arc & Year-by-Year Trajectory

In the pre-2020 official archive, Space and Science was a modest peripheral topic, totaling just 223 topic keyword mentions across more than a decade of general technology and security transcripts.[^archive-data] Dated official counts remained minimal through 2020 (2 mentions) and 2021 (29 mentions), before rising to 357 in 2022 and 305 in 2023 as space launches, James Webb Space Telescope imagery, and early commercial spaceflight stories drew sporadic attention across general-interest programs.[^archive-data]

A dramatic structural inflection arrived in 2024, when dated topic keyword mentions jumped nearly eightfold from 305 in 2023 to 2,425 in 2024.[^archive-data] That leap reflects both dense official transcript coverage of [This Week in Space](../shows/twis.md) and a landmark year of orbital milestones: Starship's orbital test flights (including dramatic re-entry footage of heat tiles burning around an aerospace flap), Boeing Starliner's crewed test flight, and intensifying debate over SpaceX's long-term Mars vehicle roadmap.[^archive-data]

Momentum climbed further in 2025 to an all-time annual peak of 2,896 mentions—driven by Starship's Flight 11 cadence, pad upgrades at Boca Chica and Kennedy Space Center, Artemis II vehicle stacking, and political debate over NASA's Artemis III moon-landing contract.[^archive-data] In 2026, dated mentions stood at 1,298, anchored by Artemis II pad readiness, Canada's Canadarm3 contribution to the lunar Gateway, Blue Origin planetary defense concepts, and Rocket Lab military testing contracts.[^archive-data] The 28-episode YouTube caption backfill contributes an additional 28 topic-level hits.[^yt-data]

# Cross-Show Editorial Coverage & Key Voices

Space coverage on TWiT is defined by veteran aerospace journalism rather than casual pop-science commentary.[^archive-data] On [This Week in Space](../shows/twis.md), co-hosts [Rod Pyle](../people/rod-pyle.md) (space historian and National Space Society editor) and [Tariq Malik](../people/tariq-malik.md) (Editor-in-Chief of Space.com) pair historical Apollo-era perspective with breaking launch-pad reporting, lightened by recurring show rituals such as the weekly space joke segment.[^archive-data] Their deep bench of recurring specialist guests elevates the technical and reporting rigor of the program: Space.com senior writer Mike Wall serves as a go-to analyst for Artemis and Starship deep dives, Ars Technica senior space editor Eric Berger brings insider reporting on SpaceX's iterative engineering culture, and veteran space journalist Leonard David provides long-horizon planetary policy analysis.[^archive-data]

Outside This Week in Space, [Leo Laporte](../people/leo-laporte.md) brings major SpaceX launches, Starlink connectivity, and scientific breakthroughs onto [This Week in Tech](../shows/twit.md) and [Intelligent Machines](../shows/im.md), while [Steve Gibson](../people/steve-gibson.md) evaluates quantum computing timelines and cryptographic implications on [Security Now](../shows/sn.md).[^archive-data]
