# Historical Arc & Year-by-Year Trajectory

No entity in the TWiT corpus exhibits a sharper leap from near-zero visibility to network-wide ubiquity than **OpenAI**.[^archive-data] Across the entire pre-2020 official archive, OpenAI appeared just **13 times**, followed by **0 in 2020**, **12 in 2021**, and **9 in 2022**.

The explosion began in **2023**, when mentions jumped nearly fiftyfold to **441**, as panels debated ChatGPT's productization (`TWIT #912`) and early cybercriminal misuse (`SN #907`). Mentions more than doubled again in **2024** to **898**, bringing 2020–2024 to **1,360 official mentions**.[^archive-data]

The **2025–2026** period accounts for **3,647 official mentions**—**61.4%** of OpenAI's official archive total.[^archive-data] Mentions peaked at **2,032 in 2025** amid AI bubble debates, revenue-versus-loss projections (`TWIT #1051`), court orders barring OpenAI from deleting user chats (`SN #1029`), and o3 reasoning models. Volume stayed high in **2026** at **1,615 mentions**, fueled by talent moves (OpenClaw creator Peter Steinberger joining OpenAI in `IM #858`), competition with [Anthropic](../entities/anthropic.md), and security alarms over agentic breakouts and OpenAI pausing "Astra" (`SN #1091`). Another **916 mentions** sit in undated (`year == 0`) official transcripts—mostly undated `Security Now` episodes—while **6** come from YouTube captions.[^archive-data][^yt-data]

# Products, Sub-Topics & Recurring Debates

The co-occurring product table traces OpenAI's expansion from a viral chatbot into reasoning models, agents, and video.[^archive-data] **OpenAI** (**3,640**) and **ChatGPT** (**2,296**) anchor the corpus. In early 2023 (`TWIT #912`), Leo Laporte quoted Yann LeCun's view that OpenAI's underlying tech was not uniquely ahead of Google or Meta, while Christina Warren countered that OpenAI won by giving the public *"a really good user interface."*

By 2025–2026, **AI Agents / Operator** surged to **1,304 mentions**, overtaking individual model families (**GPT-4 / GPT-5 / o1 / o3**, **402**) and **Sora** (**182**). On `IM #858` (Feb 18th 2026), Leo noted newer models were *"like night and day"* despite chatbot UI limits, while Emily Forlini criticized lazy "AI-to-AI" verification habits. On `SN #1091`, Steve Gibson cited [Bruce Schneier](../people/bruce-schneier.md)'s analogy equating AI agents to *"capricious genies"* after agentic breakout reports and OpenAI pausing "Astra."

**Sam Altman** (**777 mentions**) anchors governance, copyright, and financial debates: `SN #1029` covered publisher lawsuits and court-ordered chat retention, while `TWIT #1051` contrasted OpenAI's $13 billion annual revenue against projected losses of $120 billion through 2029.[^archive-data]

# Archive Methodology & Caveats
